The craft behind the shop

What is crochet?

One hook, one ball of yarn, and a fabric that grows loop by loop — no loom, no machine, no shortcuts. Here is how the craft actually works, how it differs from knitting, and what Pakistan calls it.

The short answer

Crochet is a method of building fabric by pulling continuous loops of yarn or thread through other loops with a single hooked needle — a crochet hook. Each stitch is closed and locked before the next one begins, and the word itself is French for “small hook”. Because that motion cannot be reproduced by a machine, every crochet piece in the world, anywhere, is made by hand.

Under the hook

How a stitch actually works

Four moves, repeated a few thousand times. Everything in crochet — a square, a rose, a handbag — is this sequence and nothing more.

01

The hook

One tool: a smooth shaft with a hook at the tip, sized to the yarn. It reaches into a loop, catches the thread and pulls it through. That is the whole machine.

02

The loop

Yarn is drawn through an existing loop to form a new one. Stack loops on loops and a chain appears — the opening move of nearly every piece.

03

The lock

Each stitch is finished before the next begins, so only one loop is ever live. Put the hook down and the fabric simply waits — nothing slips off.

04

The fabric

Rows grow flat panels, rounds grow tubes, and a few extra loops grow a petal. Shape is decided stitch by stitch, never cut from cloth.

Crochet vs knitting

Same yarn, different logic

The two get confused constantly, and the difference is not the yarn. It is how many stitches are left open and waiting.

Crochet

One hook, one live stitch

  • A single hooked needle does every job — no second tool, no setup.
  • Each stitch is closed before the next starts, so one loop is in play at a time.
  • Put it down mid-row and nothing unravels.
  • Firmer, more textured fabric that holds its shape — which is why bags, flowers and jewellery suit it.

Knitting

Two needles, many live stitches

  • Two or more needles hold the whole row open at once.
  • Every stitch on the needle stays live until it is worked.
  • A dropped stitch can run down the row before you catch it.
  • Thinner, drapier fabric — sweaters, scarves, socks.

Why no machine can crochet

A knitting machine holds rows of open loops on a bed of needles. Crochet gives it nothing to hold: the hook must enter a single loop from a changing angle, catch the yarn and pull it back through, with tension judged by hand at every step. No commercial machine reproduces that motion. Knitted goods can be mass-produced; true crochet cannot — so every crochet item on the market was made by a person.

Crochet in Pakistan

Here it is called Qureshia

The craft travelled under its French name and left our tongues with several of its own: Qureshia, Crosia, and a description older hands still use — ganda hua kaam, knotted work.

One craft, several names

Ask for crochet in a bazaar and you will hear three different answers. The craft itself is qureshia — people point at a lace edging and say “yeh qureshia ka kaam hai”, this is crochet work. In Punjab and in the cities the English word has been absorbed and pronounced Crosia (phonetically, the way Urdu takes on loanwords). Older generations who learned before either name settled may simply call it ganda hua kaam — knotted or braided needlework — though Qureshia stays the exact name for the tool and the technique.

The tool has its own name too: the single hooked needle is a Qureshia hook, or a Crosia ki Sui — Crosia needle. One hook, no second tool, no machine.

From the grandmother's chair to the order queue

For most of the last century, Qureshia was a domestic art taught the way recipes are: a Nani or Dadi, a hook, a ball of leftover wool, and a child's hands guided through the first chain. It furnished the home — doilies, table runners, cot covers — and it furnished weddings, where lace edgings for a bride's jahez were worked months ahead of the shaadi.

Regional taste shaped it as it moved. Artisans across Sindh, Punjab, Balochistan and Gilgit-Baltistan adapted the same hooked stitch to their own edges: fine lace borders on silk and lawn dupattas, densely worked necklines and cuffs on a kameez, flowers that never wilt on a wedding stage. The technique stayed; the motifs and the vocabulary became local.

And then the internet found it

In the last few years the craft has turned outward. What used to live in a drawer is now a small business run from a spare room: young makers sell direct on Instagram and TikTok, and home-based work brings in an income without anyone leaving the house. Gifting followed — yarn bouquets, small stuffed characters and personalised keepsakes are a routine order in Islamabad, Lahore and Karachi now, taken the way we take ours: a photo on WhatsApp, colours chosen, then stitched by hand.

We work in exactly that tradition, from a studio in Wazirabad — one hook, one piece at a time, made in the colours you ask for.

Straight answers

Crochet questions, answered

The questions people ask about the craft itself — and about ours.

What is crochet called in Pakistan?

In Pakistan, crochet is usually called Qureshia (قریشیہ) — as in “yeh qureshia ka kaam hai”, this is crochet work. In Punjab and the cities you will also hear Crosia (کروشیا), the phonetic Urdu take on the English word, while older generations may describe it as ganda hua kaam, knotted or braided needlework.

What is a Qureshia hook?

The single hooked needle used for crochet is called a Qureshia hook, or a Crosia ki Sui — Crosia needle — in everyday Urdu. It is the only tool the craft needs: a smooth shaft with a small hook that catches the yarn and pulls it through the loop.

Is crochet made by a machine?

No. True crochet is worked by hand with a single hook, and no commercial machine reproduces that motion — so every crochet piece you buy, anywhere in the world, was made by a person.

What is the difference between crochet and knitting?

Crochet uses one hook and closes each stitch before starting the next, so only one loop is ever live. Knitting holds many open stitches at once on two or more needles, which is why a dropped knitting stitch unravels down the row and a dropped crochet stitch does not.

What do you need to start crocheting?

A hook, yarn and a little patience. The hook is sized to the yarn, the yarn sets the fabric, and everything else — squares, petals, bags, flowers — grows from a single chain of loops.

How long does a crochet piece take to make?

It depends on the piece. Everything we sell is stitched after you order; the product pages show the current making time, and we confirm your date on WhatsApp before stitching begins.

Gulnish Crochet is a small studio in Wazirabad, Pakistan, where every piece is designed and stitched by hand — no machines and no mass production.
